# Employee Learning and Adoption Framework

## Principle

Employees should learn their job workflow, not the whole Suprabha OS. Routine work must remain simple enough that a new employee can become productive within one working day and should not need a manual for ordinary daily transactions after the first week.

Training must distinguish software operation from business capability. Clicking a CRM action is easy; identifying a decision maker, estimating wallet, qualifying an opportunity, diagnosing a service risk or choosing the next commercial action requires continuing development.

## Role-based learning scope

| Role | Core learning scope | Initial training target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order Desk | Customer search, order capture, priority, notes, billing handoff, exceptions | 2–3 hours |
| Warehouse / Dispatch | Action queue, packing, dispatch evidence, delivery and exceptions | 1–2 hours |
| Accounts | Tally reconciliation, receivables, credit, pricing exceptions and mismatch resolution | 3–4 hours |
| Salesperson | Customer, visit/activity, opportunity, next action, competitor/equipment and wallet intelligence | 0.5–1 day |
| Service Engineer | Instrument, service ticket, visit, work performed, parts, resolution and preventive maintenance | 2–3 hours |
| Sales Manager | Pipeline, salesperson actions, A/B accounts, wallet gaps, exceptions and coaching | ~0.5 day |
| Management | Command Center, Action Center, wallet, GP, cash, capital and drill-down | 0.5–1 day |
| System Administrator | Users/roles, configuration, corrections, audit and backup/recovery basics | 1–2 days |

These are initial training targets, not guarantees. Actual competency must be demonstrated through role-specific tasks.

## Three learning levels

### Level 1 — Operate

Learn only the transactions required for the employee's role. Target Day-1 competency for routine work.

### Level 2 — Understand

Learn why the information matters: loss reasons, competitor equipment, replacement dates, payment behaviour, service evidence, next actions and data quality. Target the first 2–4 weeks.

### Level 3 — Improve

Sales managers, senior salespeople, service leaders and management learn account strategy, wallet capture, placement economics, portfolio gaps, capital allocation and management decision-making. This is continuous development over approximately 3–6 months and beyond.

Typical progression:

Week 1: Use the system → Month 1: Understand the process → Months 2–3: Use the data → Months 3–6: Make better decisions → Long term: Build commercial expertise.

## Sales capability development

Salespeople require the deepest business-development curriculum. The future Suprabha Sales Academy should cover six capabilities:

1. Product and clinical knowledge.
2. Account intelligence.
3. Consultative selling.
4. Opportunity management.
5. Commercial and financial understanding.
6. Relationship management and negotiation.

A capable salesperson should progressively learn to understand a laboratory's testing profile, approximate volumes, methods, equipment, competitors, purchasing pattern, problems, decision makers, replacement windows, addressable wallet and realistic Suprabha capture opportunity.

## Software as a training mechanism

Suprabha OS should guide users rather than expose large blank forms. Workflows should ask concise contextual questions such as visit purpose, opportunity identified, competitor equipment observed, decision maker identified, expected replacement window, next action and due date.

Use progressive disclosure: new/routine users see only the controls needed for their work; richer commercial and management information appears only where the role and task require it.

## Capability Matrix

A future private employee Capability Matrix may track three dimensions:

- System skill: orders, CRM, wallet/intelligence, service and other role-relevant workflows.
- Domain knowledge: Biochemistry, Hematology, Molecular, Immunoassay and other relevant diagnostic divisions.
- Commercial skill: account planning, opportunity qualification, negotiation, financial understanding, collections and related capabilities.

The matrix is a coaching and training tool, not a public ranking mechanism. Scores require observable evidence and should identify the next training need rather than create false precision.

## Adoption measures

Track whether the operating model is becoming habitual, including:

- Time for a new employee to complete routine work independently.
- Training completion by role.
- Task success without assistance.
- Correction/rework rate.
- Missing mandatory business data.
- Overdue next actions.
- Percentage of work completed through the canonical workflow rather than parallel spreadsheets, notebooks or messaging.
- User-reported friction and repeated support requests.

## Guardrails

- Do not train every employee on every module.
- Do not expose management complexity in routine transaction screens.
- Do not confuse software proficiency with commercial competence.
- Do not introduce composite employee scores without transparent evidence and management-approved definitions.
- Do not use training metrics as substitutes for business outcomes.
- Prefer short workflow training plus supervised real work over long classroom sessions.
- Keep the system sufficiently intuitive that ordinary daily transactions do not depend on memorising a manual.

## Rollout approach

Before each module goes live: define the responsible role, create a short role-specific workflow, train on realistic Suprabha examples, require a small competency check, run supervised real work, capture friction, simplify the workflow, and only then broaden adoption.

Employee learning and adoption should progress alongside the Management OS build, but it must not delay the current Phase 3 pilot/hardening gate.
